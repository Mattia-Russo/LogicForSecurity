1. ATTACK_FOUND on Grant and pwd(A) secrecy — same root attack, still present
Looking at the trace carefully, the pseudonymous channel is being expanded by OFMC into its cryptographic implementation:
pseudonym(x35), {{fauth,...,pwd(x35),Na(1)}_inv(pseudonym(x35))}_(confChCr(idp))
This is OFMC's internal encoding of [A]*->*idp. The channel uses a fresh pseudonym key pseudonym(x35) for A — but crucially, A's side of the channel is signed with inv(pseudonym(x35)), a fresh private key that is not bound to any known identity. The intruder can therefore intercept the whole thing, forward it to idp, and receive the grant back — because idp has no way to verify who the pseudonymous sender is. The channel protects confidentiality of the payload (idp's confChCr(idp) key encrypts it), but the reply {fgrant,...}inv(pk(idp)) is still a bare signature that anyone can open.
This is the same structural problem as all previous weeks: Grant leaks via OpenSig on idp's signed response, and pwd(A) leaks because the pseudonymous channel does not prevent a forwarding attack — the intruder just relays the channel message wholesale.
Why it cannot be fixed at Week 5: the pseudonymous channel notation, as noted in the slides, does not provide freshness or identity binding on the client side. The intruder can always act as a relay. Resolving this would require either A having a real public key pair (outside the scenario constraints) or binding the grant response inside the TLS channel encryption — which goes beyond what OFMC's pseudonymous channel notation models.
